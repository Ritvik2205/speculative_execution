IMPORTANT CAVEAT ON PART 3 ABOVE: Part 3 calls eng.spec_flags_vector() on
each instruction IN ISOLATION (the old single-instruction regex path). But
apply_dataflow_taint() (spec/dataflow_taint.py) mutates node.spec_flags AFTER
a full PDG is built (spec/spec_pdg_builder.py, SpecBackedPDGBuilder.build()),
which is what the GINE classifier actually consumes at eval time. Part 3's
0.000% is_secret_source / is_transmitter numbers above therefore do NOT test
whether dataflow_taint helped -- they only show the pre-dataflow_taint,
single-instruction-regex signal (which is expected to be 0% on RISC-V by
design, since riscv.json's indexed-addressing regex is a placeholder).

To get the number that actually matters -- does dataflow_taint raise these
rates in the graphs the classifier sees -- this session built real PDGs via
SpecBackedPDGBuilder(riscv_engine) with dataflow_taint on vs off and counted
node-level flag rates directly (script:

CONCLUSION: dataflow_taint does fire on RISC-V now (0.000%->0.100%/0.150%
node-rate, up from a hard structural zero), confirming the spec-engine fix
itself works as designed. But it fires almost entirely on SPECTRE_RSB (100%
of its 4 records) -- the one class G6 already identified as having a real,
uncontaminated RISC-V-native attack primitive -- and essentially NEVER on
L1TF (0/162) or MDS (0/36), the two classes the fix was meant to rescue.
This is fully consistent with G6's corpus-contamination finding: L1TF/MDS's
actual leak-mechanism instructions in riscv_corpus/*.s are still verbatim
ARM64 mnemonics (ldr/dc civac/ldrb/lsl) that riscv.json correctly does not
recognize as RISC-V opcodes, so there is no RISC-V LOAD->SHIFT->LOAD chain
for dataflow_taint to find in those records regardless of how well the
spec-engine mechanism itself works. The spec-engine fix is real and correct;
it just has nothing to attach to for the two classes that matter most, on
this corpus.
