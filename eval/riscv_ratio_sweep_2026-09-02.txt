RISC-V attack:benign RATIO SWEEP — operating curve (2026-09-02)
==============================================================
Fixed 37 idiomatic riscv ATTACK gadgets; varied idiomatic riscv BENIGN {0,20,40,80}.
2 seeds each, held-out real(n=11)/synth(n=358)/benign(n=180) never trained on.
benign FP = 100 - benign recall. baseline (no riscv data): attack 0%, FP 36.7%.

  benign  real attack   synth attack   benign FP     locked
   0      36-73%        66-70%         98-100%       93.5/95.8
   20     36-64%        60-67%         73-82%        95.3/94.6
   40     54-64%        20-61%         64-80%        95.8/95.3
   80     18-55%        34%            21-30%        95.4/94.7   <== only config below baseline FP

READING THE CURVE:
- Attack recall stays HIGH (synth 60-67%, real 36-64%) up through benign=40, but
  benign FP stays ABOVE the 37% baseline until benign jumps to 80.
- The FP only crosses below baseline at ~2:1 benign:attack (80:37) -> FP 21-30%,
  and that is the point where BOTH axes beat baseline. It costs attack recall
  (synth 66% -> 34%).
- So there is a sharp FP transition between 40 and 80 benign; the sweet spot for
  "dominates baseline on both axes" is ~60-80 benign. Pushing benign past 80 would
  likely drop FP further at more attack-recall cost -- but needs MORE benign data
  (only 80 available).

OPERATING-POINT CONCLUSION:
- Low-FP deployment (FP < baseline): benign=80 (2:1). Attack real 18-55% / synth 34%,
  FP 21-30%, locked ~95.
- High-recall / triage (FP not the priority): benign=20-40. Attack real 36-64% /
  synth 60-67%, but FP 64-82% (worse than baseline) -- only sensible when a second
  filter follows.

HONEST CAVEATS: seed variance is LARGE (synth at benign=40: 20 vs 61; real at 20:
36 vs 64) -- n=11 real is 3-7 gadgets and 2 seeds cannot pin the curve. This maps
the SHAPE (more benign -> lower FP, lower attack recall) and locates the baseline-
dominating point (~80), but the exact numbers need >=5 seeds and larger held-out
sets. Not a final tuned curve.

THE FP FLOOR: yes, more benign pushes FP down to ~5-13% (benign 120-200) -- BUT real
attack recall COLLAPSES there (0-18%). Past ~80 benign the model swings back to a
benign-default; the FP win is bought with the attack detection we came for.

DEFINITIVE OPERATING POINT: benign ~= 80 (2:1 benign:attack) is the knee -- the point
that beats the 36.7% baseline FP (21-30%) while keeping real attack recall meaningfully
above 0 (18-55%). Below 80 -> FP > baseline; above 80 -> attack recall craters. The
curve is U-shaped in usefulness, minimised near 80.

Locked test holds ~95 across the whole sweep (the riscv data barely touches x86/arm).

CAVEATS unchanged: n=11 real = 3-7 gadgets, large seed variance (real at 120: 0 vs 9;
synth at 200: 34 vs 46), 2 seeds. The SHAPE and the knee location (~80) are the
robust findings; exact per-point numbers need >=5 seeds + a larger real held-out set.
